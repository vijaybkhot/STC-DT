# Ecosystem Roadmap

This roadmap describes proposal-stage directions rather than guaranteed deliverables. Priorities and sequencing remain subject to contributor confirmation, research findings, available resources, and governance decisions.

## Proposal stage

- Complete confirmation of the AI-Enabled Geometry component when Ehsan provides its public release information; the Knowledge Graph, Urban Digital Twin, and CCDT registry entries are already verified.
- Maintain the umbrella repository as the public documentation and coordination entry point.
- Preserve explicit labels for current, planned, experimental, and proposed work.
- Identify initial interoperability use cases without assuming a technical solution.
- Maintain Apache-2.0 licensing and minimum contributor, conduct, and security documentation.
- Record how cross-component decisions will be proposed, reviewed, and adopted.
- Complete a proposal-readiness review for factual accuracy, links, diagrams, and confidential-material exclusions.

## Near-term ecosystem goals

- Document representative component inputs, outputs, formats, and boundaries.
- Identify overlaps, gaps, and dependencies across the four component domains.
- Define requirements for provenance, metadata, validation, security, and privacy.
- Investigate candidate shared interface or exchange conventions.
- Establish versioning, compatibility, and change-review practices.
- Develop small interoperability examples only after the relevant component facts, interfaces, and exchange requirements are confirmed.

## Longer-term ecosystem goals

- Evaluate proposed protocols through cross-component research use cases.
- Publish stable interoperability guidance where evidence supports it.
- Develop conformance and validation practices for adopted interfaces.
- Coordinate security response and maintenance responsibilities across repositories.
- Support contributor onboarding across institutions and disciplines.
- Establish a sustainable governance, release, and stewardship model.

## Roadmap status

No roadmap item should be interpreted as implemented unless supporting documentation identifies its responsible contributors, evidence, and current status.
